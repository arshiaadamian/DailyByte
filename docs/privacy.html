Privacy Policy

DailyByte sends you one short thing worth knowing each day. To do that it needs to know your email, what you want to learn about, and when to reach you. That is close to everything it stores.

Last updated 21 September 2026  ·  Effective 21 September 2026

The short version

No advertising, no analytics SDKs, no third-party trackers, and nothing sold or shared for marketing. You can delete your account and everything attached to it from inside the app at any time.

01Who this covers

DailyByte is an independent app built and operated by a single developer. This policy explains what the app collects, why, where it goes, and what you can do about it. It applies to the DailyByte iOS app and the service behind it.

02What is collected, and what is not

When you create an account

Your email address. If you sign up directly, you give it to us along with a password, which is stored and verified by Amazon Cognito — the app never sees or holds it. If you sign in with Apple or Google, they confirm your identity and pass along your email address; your password stays with them and is never shared with us.

If you use Sign in with Apple and choose Hide My Email, we receive a relay address ending in privaterelay.appleid.com rather than your real one. That works exactly the same way for us, and your real address stays with Apple.

When you set up your delivery

Your chosen topic, how many bytes per day you want, the times you want them, and your time zone. The time zone is read from your device setting so that "8:00 AM" means your 8:00 AM.

When you enable notifications

A push token — an anonymous identifier issued by Apple that lets a notification reach your device. It is not your phone number, your device ID, or anything that identifies you outside this app, and it changes if you reinstall.

As you use the app

The bytes generated for you are stored so you can read your history, and so that tomorrow's byte can build on what you have already seen rather than repeat it.

What is never collected

  • No location data
  • No contacts, photos, calendar, or health data
  • No advertising identifier, and no advertising of any kind
  • No analytics or crash-reporting SDKs, and no behavioural profiling
  • No cross-app or cross-site tracking

03How your information is used

Only to run the service you signed up for: authenticating you, generating bytes on your topic, delivering them at your chosen times, and showing you your history. Nothing is used for marketing, advertising, or profiling, and none of it is sold, rented, or shared with data brokers.

04Where it is stored

All account data, preferences and bytes are stored on Amazon Web Services in the Canada West (Calgary) region, using Amazon Cognito for accounts and Amazon DynamoDB for preferences and byte history. Data is encrypted in transit and at rest, and every request between the app and the service travels over HTTPS.

05How your bytes are generated

Each byte is written by a large language model — Anthropic's Claude, accessed through Amazon Bedrock — prompted with your chosen topic and the titles of bytes you have already received, so it can build on them.

Generated content can be wrong. Bytes are short, automatically written, and not reviewed by a person before they reach you. Treat them as a starting point for curiosity, not as fact — and never as medical, legal, or financial advice. Each byte includes a search link so you can check it yourself.

Your topic and recent byte titles are sent to Amazon Bedrock to produce each byte. Your email address is not. Per Amazon's terms for Bedrock, prompts are not used to train the underlying models.

06Who else is involved

DailyByte relies on a small number of services, each doing one job:

Service Role
Amazon Web Services Accounts, data storage, scheduling, and byte generation
Apple Push notification delivery, and Sign in with Apple if you use it
Google Sign in with Google, if you use it
Expo Relays notifications from the service to Apple's network

Each receives only what it needs to do its job. None of them receive your byte history, and none are given your information for their own marketing.

07Your choices

Change what you receive

Your topic, how many bytes a day, and their delivery times can all be changed at any time under Settings in the app.

Stop notifications

Turn off notifications for DailyByte in your device's iOS Settings. Your account stays intact and your bytes keep building in your history.

Delete your account

Under Settings → Delete account. This is immediate and permanent, and it removes:

  • Your account and sign-in credentials
  • Your email address, topic, schedule and time zone
  • Every byte ever generated for you
  • Your push token and all scheduled deliveries

There is no recovery period and no backup copy kept for you. If you want to come back afterwards, you will be starting fresh.

08How long things are kept

Your account and preferences are kept for as long as your account exists. Bytes are kept so you can read your history. Everything is removed when you delete your account. Operational logs, which record that a request happened rather than what was in it, are retained for a short period for debugging and then discarded.

09Children

DailyByte is not directed at children and is not intended for anyone under 13. We do not knowingly collect information from children. If you believe a child has created an account, get in touch and it will be removed.

10Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to access, correct, export, or delete the information held about you. Most of this is available directly in the app: Settings shows and edits your preferences, and Delete account removes everything. For anything else, contact us using the details below and we will respond.
